# Mai Chau: a valley that does not have to try

Mai Chau has no highest peak, no most-spectacular waterfall, and nothing to climb. What it has is a flat valley floor between two limestone walls, rice fields stitched together by bamboo footbridges, and White Thai stilt houses standing in the middle of the fields.

That is exactly why it works for first-time travellers, for families with small children, and for anyone with two free days who does not want to spend most of them in a vehicle. This Mai Chau travel guide covers the valley itself — where to sleep, when the rice turns, and how to get there from Hanoi without losing a day.

## Where Mai Chau is — and which province it belongs to now

About 135 km west of Hanoi, via National Route 6 over the Thung Khe pass and down into the valley.

> **Administrative update (2026):** on 1 July 2025 Hoa Binh province was merged into Phu Tho. Mai Chau is now **Mai Chau commune, Phu Tho province** (it was Mai Chau district, Hoa Binh). Lac village, Pom Coong, Na Phon and Thung Khe all now sit inside the same commune.

The valley is mostly White Thai, alongside Kinh and Muong families. Higher up on the surrounding slopes are Hmong villages, including the one that hosts the Pa Co market.

## When to go

| Window | Conditions | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| **February – April** | 20–28°C, morning mist | Flooded paddies like mirrors, plum and peach blossom |
| **May** | Hot, 28–34°C | Vivid green after transplanting, dramatic afternoon storm light |
| **September – October** | 22–30°C, dry | Rice turning gold — the classic photographs |
| **June – August** | 30–36°C, heavy rain | Lush but muddy; cheapest homestays |

**The two windows worth planning around:** late September to mid-October for golden rice, and February to March for flooded paddies and cool mornings. If you go in June–August, expect high humidity and slippery clay paths.

One easily missed detail: Mai Chau fills with domestic weekend groups, especially on Saturday night. If you want quiet, go Sunday afternoon to Friday.

## What to see

### Lac village

The oldest and busiest stilt-house village, right beside the main road. This is where most homestays, restaurants and weaving families are concentrated, with looms set up under the floorboards.

Walking the village takes one to two hours. If you want to photograph someone weaving, buy a small piece first — it is both fairer and friendlier.

### Pom Coong and Van village

Two quieter hamlets next to Lac. Pom Coong is now a residential quarter of Mai Chau commune; Van sits deeper in the valley and has a bamboo bridge crossing the stream.

Cycling to Van in the morning, eating lunch there and returning before dusk is a very good use of a half day.

### Thung Khe pass

A white limestone pass on National Route 6, known for roadside stalls selling grilled corn and sugarcane. Stop on the Mai Chau side for the view down into the valley.

Mist often covers the pass in the early morning and can cut visibility sharply. If you are driving yourself, slow down here.

### Pa Co market

A Hmong upland market held on Sunday mornings, about 30 km of mountain road from the valley. This is a real market rather than a tourist one: horses, pigs, indigo textiles, corn wine, herbal medicine.

Leave at 6:30 am. Bring cash and fill your tank — there are stretches with no phone signal.

### Mo Luong cave

A limestone cave with lighting, a few kilometres from the centre. The steps are steep and often wet, so wear shoes rather than sandals.

### Go Lao waterfall and the Hoa Binh reservoir

A modest waterfall and a stretch of the Da River reservoir where you can hire kayaks or boats. Good for an afternoon, especially when water levels are high.

### The valley cycling loop

Flat, and roughly 12–15 km if you link the villages. This is the single best thing to do in Mai Chau, because it puts you among the fields, the buffalo and the actual pace of the valley.

- Bicycle hire: 50,000–90,000 VND per day; e-bikes around 150,000–200,000 VND.
- Best light: 6–8 am and 4–6 pm.
- Do not try to cycle between 11 am and 3 pm in summer.

## A three-day plan

| Day | Morning | Afternoon | Evening |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Leave Hanoi at 7:30, stop at Thung Khe, arrive around 11 am | Check in, cycle the valley loop | Family dinner, rice wine if offered |
| **Day 2** | Pa Co market (if Sunday) or Mo Luong cave | Go Lao waterfall, kayak on the reservoir | Night walk through the paddies |
| **Day 3** | Cycle to Van village for breakfast, bamboo bridge | Weaving or cooking with a host family | Bus back to Hanoi by 4 pm |

With only two days, drop Pa Co and Go Lao. Keep one cycling session and one evening in the village. Mai Chau does not reward rushing between sights.

## Where to stay

| Area | Atmosphere | Best for | Typical rate/night |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lac village homestays** | Stilt houses, shared mattresses, family meals | Budget, groups, sociable travellers | US$10–20 |
| **Pom Coong / Van village** | Quieter stilt houses, private rooms | Couples, light sleepers | US$15–38 |
| **Eco-lodges around Na Phon** | Bungalows in the fields, pool | Comfort, honeymoons | US$85–190 |
| **Guesthouses in the centre** | Standard rooms, hot water, air conditioning | Families, short stays | US$25–60 |

Ask one question before you book: private room or shared mattress? Traditional stilt homestays often mean sleeping in a communal space, which is part of the appeal for some travellers and a dealbreaker for others.

## Practical notes

- **Getting there:** limousine vans from Hanoi take 3–3.5 hours, roughly 200,000–300,000 VND per person. Public buses from My Dinh are cheaper but slower and stop more often.
- **Cash:** there are very few ATMs in the valley and they often fail. Withdraw in Hanoi before you leave.
- **Homestay etiquette:** shoes off at the stairs, do not step on the sleeping-mat area, and ask before photographing inside a house or close up on faces.
- **Dress:** shoulders and knees covered in villages and at any shrine. People live here; it is not a set.
- **Cycling:** village lanes have small children and free-roaming dogs. Ride slowly and do not lean on the horn.
- **Rice season:** the fields belong to real families. Do not walk into the middle of a paddy for a photo without asking.

## FAQ

**How many days do I need?**
Two days and one night covers a cycling session and an evening in the village. Three days adds Pa Co market or Go Lao waterfall.

**Do I need a guide?**
No. The valley is flat, the roads are clear, and homestay hosts give good directions.

**Is Pa Co market worth it?**
Yes, if you are there on a Sunday and accept 30 km of mountain road. It is a genuinely local market, quite different from the tourist-facing villages in the valley.

**Is it good for young children?**
Very. The terrain is flat, there is no climbing, and children tend to enjoy the cycling and the buffalo coming home.

**When are the best photographs?**
September–October for golden rice, February–March for mirror-flat flooded paddies. Both need early mornings.
