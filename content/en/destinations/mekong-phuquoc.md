# Mekong Delta and Phu Quoc: From Floating Market Dawn to Island Sunset

Southern Vietnam offers one journey that justifies the long flight on its own: two or three days moving at water speed through the delta, then a fast boat and a completely different country — white sand, warm sea, and an island where the main road runs beside the beach for twenty kilometres.

The trick is the order. Do the delta first, while you still have the energy for 5 a.m. starts, then let Phu Quoc do nothing to you for four days. This Mekong Delta and Phu Quoc itinerary is built on that sequence, with the ferries, the markets and the beaches placed where they actually fit.

## The shape of the trip

| | |
|---|---|
| **Route** | Ho Chi Minh City → Ben Tre / Can Tho → Rach Gia or Ha Tien → Phu Quoc |
| **Minimum** | 5 days: 2 delta + 3 island |
| **Best months** | November to April (dry, calm sea); September to November for the flood season |
| **Getting to the island** | Ferry 1 h 15 – 2 h 30, or a 1-hour flight from Ho Chi Minh City |
| **Money** | Cash for markets and ferries; cards in island resorts |

## When to go

The delta and the island share a calendar but not a mood.

- **November – April**: dry, bright, low humidity. The delta's canals are navigable and the island's west coast gets its sunsets. This is the window to book.
- **September – November**: the flood season (*mùa nước nổi*). Water rises, canals that are dry the rest of the year become roads, and the delta looks like the photographs. Rice fields turn gold in November.
- **May – October**: hot, with heavy afternoon rain. Green, cheap, quieter, and Phu Quoc still gets long stretches of morning sun.
- **Avoid if you can**: Tet (late January or February, depending on the lunar year), 30 April – 1 May, and 2 September. Ferries and hotels sell out weeks ahead and prices double.

## The floating markets, ranked by what you actually want

All of them start before sunrise. By 8 a.m. the wholesale trade is finished and only tour boats remain.

| Market | Where | Time | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cai Rang** | Can Tho | 5:30–7:00 | The big one: hundreds of boats, noodle soup and coffee cooked on deck. Touristy, and still worth it once. |
| **Phong Dien** | Can Tho | 5:00–6:30 | Smaller, earlier, more wholesale. Pair it with Cai Rang rather than choosing. |
| **Cai Be** | My Tho area, 2 h from Saigon | 5:00–7:30 | The easiest day trip; fruit and vegetables, winding canals, good for a first taste |
| **Long Xuyen** | An Giang | 5:00–7:00 | Fewer tourists, harder to reach, more genuine trading |
| **Nga Bay** | Hau Giang | Early | A wholesale hub rather than a photo stop |
| **Tra On** | Vinh Long | Early | Fruit specialists; quiet and unhurried |

The detail that makes a floating market legible: each boat hangs a sample of its cargo from a long pole, so you know what is for sale before you pull alongside.

**Cai Rang practicalities.** Stay in Can Tho the night before, leave the dock at 5:15, and take a small rowing boat for the last stretch — the big tour boats cannot enter the narrow channels where the real trade happens. Expect to pay for the boat per person, and bring small notes.

## What else the delta does well

- **Ben Tre** — coconut country. Candy workshops, coconut oil, canals too narrow for coaches, and the best place to understand how the delta makes a living.
- **Sa Dec** — flower villages along the river and the Huynh Thuy Le house, the setting behind Marguerite Duras's *The Lover*.
- **Tra Su cajuput forest** — a flooded forest crossed by sampan, with a tower over the treetops. Best in the flood season, when the water is high and the birds are in.
- **Chau Doc** — Sam Mountain, the Ba Chua Xu temple, and a mix of Khmer, Cham, Chinese and Kinh communities that explains the delta better than any museum.
- **U Minh Thuong** — peat swamp and birdlife, for travellers who have already done the markets.

## Getting from the delta to Phu Quoc

> **Administrative update (2026):** since 1 July 2025 Kien Giang has been merged into **An Giang province**, so Phu Quoc is now the **Phu Quoc special zone (dac khu Phu Quoc), An Giang**. Rach Gia and Ha Tien — the two ports below — are wards of An Giang as well. The crossings and timetables are unchanged.

Two mainland ports, two different experiences.

| From | Crossing | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rach Gia** | About 2 h 20 – 2 h 30 by fast boat | 300,000–350,000 VND | The default from Can Tho; 3–3.5 h by road to the port |
| **Ha Tien** | About 1 h 15 – 1 h 30 by fast boat | 220,000–260,000 VND | Closest crossing; convenient from Chau Doc and the Cambodian border |
| **Phu Quoc by air** | 1 h from Ho Chi Minh City, 2 h from Hanoi | From about 700,000 VND | Fastest, and the only way to use the island's visa exemption |

Every boat lands at **Bai Vong port** on the east coast — not at Duong Dong, and not near the beaches. Factor in a 20–30 minute transfer to the hotel strip.

Book the morning boats, keep a buffer day before any international flight, and check the weather the night before: northeast winds in November and December do cancel sailings.

## Phu Quoc: what the island is actually like

Phu Quoc is Vietnam's largest island, roughly 570 km², about 45 km off the coast at Ha Tien. It has changed fast — there is now a cable car, a theme park and a safari — but the south and the north still feel like an island.

**Beaches**

- **Long Beach (Bai Truong)** — the west-coast strip, a long run of sand facing sunset, lined with resorts and seafood restaurants. Convenient rather than wild.
- **Sao Beach (Bai Sao)** — the postcard: powder sand, shallow turquoise water, starfish offshore. Busy from 10 a.m.; go at 8.
- **Khem Beach (Bai Kem)** — a protected bay in the south with calm water and the island's most expensive hotels.
- **Ganh Dau and the north** — empty sand, fishing villages, and the least development on the island.

**Beyond the beach**

- **Hon Thom cable car** — nearly 7.9 km across the sea to Hon Thom island, one of the longest non-stop cable car routes in the world. The ride is the attraction; the island end has a water park.
- **An Thoi islands** — snorkelling and diving trips from An Thoi pier; coral is best at Hon Doi Moi and Half Moon Reef. Visibility is limited in the rainy season.
- **Ham Ninh fishing village** — stilt houses, sunrise, and seafood served on the dock at 7 a.m.
- **Phu Quoc Prison** — the French-era and American-war prison, with the tiger cages intact. Sobering, and the island's most important historical site.
- **Duong Dong night market** — grilled seafood, pearl sellers, sim wine, and the island's peppercorn and fish sauce on sale by the bottle.
- **Pepper farms and fish sauce houses** — Phu Quoc fish sauce is barrel-fermented and protected by geographical indication; a 30-minute visit explains why it tastes different.

## Two itineraries that work

**Five days (the classic)**
Day 1: Saigon → Ben Tre, coconut canals, night in a homestay.
Day 2: early transfer to Can Tho, Cai Rang at 5:30, then the 3.5-hour drive to Rach Gia and the afternoon boat to Phu Quoc.
Days 3–5: beach, one boat trip to the An Thoi islands, night market, and the cable car if the sky is clear.

**Seven days (the version worth taking)**
Add Sa Dec and Chau Doc, sunrise at Tra Su, a night on Sam Mountain, and two extra island days with a scooter.

## Where to stay

**Delta**: a riverside homestay in Ben Tre or an old villa in Can Tho. Air conditioning matters more than charm in April.

**Phu Quoc**: Duong Dong for markets and budget, Long Beach for resorts and sunsets, Sao or Khem for quiet luxury, the north for eco-lodges and empty roads.

## Practical notes, including the visa rule people get wrong

- **The Phu Quoc visa exemption** allows most nationalities 30 days without a visa — but only if you arrive on the island directly from abroad, or transit airside through a Vietnamese airport without entering the country. If you spend three days in Saigon first, you need a visa or e-visa for Vietnam.
- **Cash** for markets, ferries and small restaurants. ATMs exist on the island but are sparse in the delta.
- **Mosquito repellent** for delta evenings; the river is beautiful and the mosquitoes agree.
- **Motion sickness** for the Rach Gia crossing, which is open sea for most of its length. Take something 30 minutes before boarding.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen** for snorkelling, and a rash shirt — the sun on the water is stronger than it feels.
- **Plastic**: the delta's canals have a waste problem. Carry a refillable bottle; several hotels now refill.

## FAQ

**Can I do the delta as a day trip from Saigon?**
Yes — My Tho and Ben Tre are 2 hours away and make a long but manageable day. You will see canals and coconut workshops, not a real floating market at its best.

**Which floating market is the most authentic?**
Long Xuyen and Phong Dien. Cai Rang is the most impressive and the most crowded; both statements are true at once.

**Do I need to book the ferry in advance?**
In peak season and around public holidays, yes — days ahead. Off-season you can usually buy at the port.

**Is Phu Quoc expensive?**
It ranges from 400,000 VND guesthouses to 15 million VND villas. Food and drink are reasonable outside resort restaurants.

**Can I combine Phu Quoc with Cambodia?**
Yes: Ha Tien is on the border, and the ferry route makes a natural link. Check visa requirements for both countries before you commit.

**How many days on the island?**
Three is the minimum for beach plus one boat trip. Five is where it starts to feel like a holiday.
