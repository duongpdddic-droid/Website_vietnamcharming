# Mui Ne: dunes, wind, and mornings that start before the sun

Mui Ne is a thin strip of coast where the sand reaches almost to the water and the wind rarely stops. People come for three things: dunes that change colour by the hour, a sea flat enough and windy enough to fly a kite on, and a fishing harbour that still launches boats every night as if no resort had ever been built beside it.

This Mui Ne travel guide covers what actually matters for two or three days — when to go, what to see, where to sleep, what to eat, and the small practical details that decide whether the trip feels easy or exhausting.

## Where Mui Ne is, and why it is worth the trip

Mui Ne sits at the eastern edge of the old Binh Thuan region, about 22–25 km from the centre of Phan Thiet.

> **Place-name note (2026):** on 1 July 2025 Binh Thuan was merged into Lam Dong province. Mui Ne, Ham Tien and Thien Nghiep were consolidated into **Mui Ne ward, Lam Dong province**. This guide still uses "Phan Thiet" for the central urban area because that is how everyone refers to it, but newer maps and paperwork will show Mui Ne ward and Phan Thiet ward.

Three landscapes sit within 30 km of each other:

- **Dunes** — Bau Trang (white, wide, serious) and the Red Dunes (smaller, closer, better at sunset).
- **Fairy Stream** — a shallow creek cutting through red and white clay walls; you walk it barefoot.
- **Beach and harbour** — a long swimming beach, round basket boats, drying racks of squid.

The honest drawback: Mui Ne has no major monuments and no museum worth a detour. It is a place for being outdoors, not for going indoors.

## When to go: pick wind or pick calm

| Window | Conditions | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| November – March | Dry, strong steady wind, 24–31°C | Kitesurfers, anyone who likes cool air |
| April – May | Hot at 28–34°C, wind drops, sea goes flat | Swimming, dune photography, families |
| June – October | 26–32°C, short heavy showers, lowest room rates | Budget travellers, storm-light photographers |

**If you only get one trip:** aim for late December to March. The wind is strong enough for a beginner to learn, the air is dry, and the dunes look their best at sunrise.

**A note for new kitesurfers:** the wind builds through the early afternoon. Book a morning lesson rather than a 3 pm one — the difference between learning and being dragged is often just the hour.

June to October is the wet season, but showers usually last one or two hours, mostly in the late afternoon. In exchange, room rates fall and the beach is noticeably quieter.

## What to see

### Bau Trang (the white dunes)

About 25–30 km northeast of Mui Ne, in Hoa Thang commune. This is the biggest dune field in the area, with a freshwater lake below it fringed with lotus.

- **Best hours:** 5:00–7:00 am. By 8 am the sand is hot and the shadows have flattened out.
- **Getting there:** jeep or private car via the Hon Rom road; many shared tours leave at 4:30 am.
- **Bring:** water, a hat with a chin strap, a scarf. There is no shade on the dunes.

### The Red Dunes

Smaller than Bau Trang but close to the hotel strip, which makes them ideal for a late-afternoon visit. Local families rent plastic sleds at the foot of the slope.

Do not expect solitude. This is the busiest spot at sunset and you will be offered a sled repeatedly.

### Fairy Stream

A shallow stream running between clay walls in shades of red, white and orange. Walk upstream about 20 minutes for the best section.

- Go barefoot or in strapped sandals; wet clay is slippery.
- Visit early to avoid the heat bouncing off the walls.
- There is a small entry fee, typically a few tens of thousands of dong.

### Mui Ne fishing harbour

The basket boats come in roughly between 5:00 and 7:00 am. That is the moment worth setting an alarm for: boats packed along the sand, squid and fish on drying racks, buyers and sellers working directly on the beach.

Ask before photographing faces up close. A nod and a smile is enough in most cases.

### Po Sah Inu Cham towers

A group of Cham brick towers on Ba Nai hill, built in the late 8th century and dedicated to Shiva. The brick takes on its deepest colour between about 4:30 and 5:30 pm.

Dress code: shoulders and knees covered. This is still an active religious site, not a photo set.

### Ke Ga lighthouse

Built of granite blocks by the French, started in February 1897, finished at the end of 1898 and first lit in 1900. The tower is 35 m tall, the light sits 65 m above the sea, and 183 spiral steps lead to the top.

It is roughly 30 km south of Phan Thiet. The path to the islet crosses a rocky causeway that floods with the tide — check tide times, and do not attempt the crossing once the water is up.

## A three-day itinerary that actually works

| Day | Morning | Afternoon | Evening |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Arrive from Saigon, check in | Swim, or take a kite lesson if the wind is up | Seafood at the harbour, walk the night market |
| **Day 2** | 4:30 am to Bau Trang for sunrise, stop at Fairy Stream on the way back | Rest, then the Red Dunes at 4:00 pm | Early dinner, early night |
| **Day 3** | Dawn at the fishing harbour, then Po Sah Inu | Dragon fruit farm or Ke Ga lighthouse | Night bus or train back to Saigon |

With a fourth day, restructure it: give Ke Ga a full morning (go early, return before high tide) and spend another afternoon on a scooter following the coast road out to Hon Rom.

## Where to stay

| Area | Atmosphere | Best for | Typical rate/night |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nguyen Dinh Chieu km 12–16** | Resorts with pools, kite schools next door | Kitesurfers, couples | US$35–120 |
| **Ham Tien (now Ham Tien quarters)** | Small guesthouses, cheap restaurants, bars | Budget travellers, solo | US$12–32 |
| **Central Phan Thiet** | Markets, local food, real city rhythm | Food-focused, longer stays | US$18–48 |
| **Beachfront km 18–24** | Large resorts, private sand, spa | Families, honeymoons | US$140–400 |

The short version: **sleep in Ham Tien, eat in Phan Thiet, kite in Mui Ne.** The three areas are 10–15 minutes apart by scooter but differ sharply in price.

## What to eat

| Dish | Where | Typical price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Banh can** | Alley stalls in Phan Thiet, morning | 30,000–50,000 VND | Small rice cakes baked in clay moulds, served with dipping sauce and meatballs |
| **Banh xeo Phan Thiet** | Local eateries | 25,000–40,000 VND | Small, crisp, heavy on shrimp and bean sprouts |
| **Banh canh cha ca** | Phan Thiet market | 30,000–45,000 VND | Thick noodles, clear broth, fish cake — the local breakfast |
| **Grilled seafood** | Shacks near the harbour | 200,000–400,000 VND | Pick from the tank; priced per kilo live weight |
| **Muoi mot nang (one-sun squid)** | Markets, roadside | 350,000–600,000 VND/kg | Pressed and dried for a single sunny day; cheaper at the market than at the airport |
| **Phan Thiet fish sauce** | Producers in town | 60,000–150,000 VND/bottle | Barrel-aged 12–18 months; buy at source |
| **Dragon fruit** | Markets, roadside | 20,000–40,000 VND/kg | Red flesh is sweeter and cheaper here than almost anywhere else |

One habit worth adopting: seafood is always priced per kilo of **live weight**. Ask for the total and the cooking charge before you agree, not after the plates are empty.

## Getting there

- **Limousine van / sleeper bus:** 4–5 hours from Ho Chi Minh City, door to door, roughly 250,000–400,000 VND per person.
- **Train:** the Saigon–Phan Thiet service takes about 3.5–4 hours, then a 12–15 km taxi or motorbike ride to Mui Ne. Schedules change seasonally, so confirm before booking.
- **Motorbike:** about 200 km via National Route 1A, then the Nguyen Dinh Chieu coast road. Doable, but split it into two stages if you are not used to long-distance riding.
- **Getting around:** a rented scooter at 120,000–180,000 VND per day is the most flexible option. Ride-hailing apps work but are thin at dawn when the dune tours leave.

## Practical notes

- **Cash:** ATMs exist along Nguyen Dinh Chieu but run dry at weekends. Carry one to two million dong in small notes for entry fees, parking and sled rental.
- **Sand:** it gets into everything. A zip bag for your phone and camera is the cheapest useful thing you will buy.
- **Swimming safety:** the beach shelves quickly and there are currents. Swim where other people are, and not alone at dusk.
- **Sun:** between 10 am and 3 pm the glare off the sand is brutal. Polarised sunglasses and high-SPF sunscreen are equipment, not accessories.
- **Dress:** shoulders and knees covered at Po Sah Inu and at pagodas. Swimwear belongs on the beach.

## FAQ

**How many days do I need in Mui Ne?**
Two days covers the dunes and the harbour. Three gives you room for Ke Ga or a proper kite lesson.

**Can I learn kitesurfing if I cannot swim?**
No. Kitesurfing requires swimming ability and water confidence. Learn to swim first, or choose paddleboarding and beach-level kite flying instead.

**Is Mui Ne worth visiting in the rainy season?**
Yes, with adjusted expectations. Rain is usually short and late in the day; mornings stay usable. Room rates drop noticeably.

**Should I book a jeep tour or hire a car?**
For one or two people, a shared tour is cheaper. From three people up, a private car is usually better value and lets you control the timing.

**Is it suitable for small children?**
Yes, but go early. The sand becomes too hot for bare feet by mid-morning and young children tire fast in the heat.
