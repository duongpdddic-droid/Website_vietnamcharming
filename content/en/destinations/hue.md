# Hue: The Imperial City, Its Tombs, and the Slow River Between Them

Hue was the capital of Vietnam from 1802 to 1945, and the Nguyen dynasty left behind a walled city, seven royal tombs scattered along the Perfume River, and a court cuisine that still shapes what the whole country eats.

It is also one of the wettest cities in Vietnam, which is not a footnote. Hue rewards people who plan around water: mornings for monuments, afternoons for food and cafés, and a flexible day if the river rises. This Hue travel guide works to that order, with the ticket combinations and the hours that keep you out of the worst of it.

## Hue at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Central Vietnam, on the Perfume (Huong) River; a centrally-run city since January 2025 |
| **Imperial period** | 1802–1945, thirteen Nguyen emperors |
| **Best months** | January to April |
| **Rainy season** | September to December, heaviest October–November |
| **Minimum stay** | 2 nights; 3 if you add the countryside and a cooking class |
| **Getting there** | 2–2.5 hours by train from Da Nang over the Hai Van Pass; Phu Bai airport 15 km away |

## When to go

| Period | Conditions | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Jan – Mar** | 18–25 °C, mostly dry, occasional drizzle | The best window: cool enough to walk the citadel at midday |
| **Apr – May** | 26–33 °C, sunny, humid | Good, but start monuments at 7 a.m. |
| **Jun – Aug** | 33–38 °C, dry with thunderstorms | Beach weather at Thuan An, hard sightseeing weather |
| **Sep – Nov** | Heavy rain, flooding in low areas | Atmospheric and cheap, but plan indoors and check forecasts |
| **Dec** | Cool, wet, 18–22 °C | Quiet, good for food, poor for photography |

Hue's rainfall is genuinely different from the rest of the coast: the Truong Son range squeezes moisture out of northeast winds and dumps it here between September and December. If your trip is flexible, come in February or March.

## The monuments, and what they cost

Tickets are sold individually or as combos valid for two days. Buy at the citadel ticket office and keep the paper — it is checked at each site.

| Ticket | Price (adult) | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Imperial City only** | 200,000 VND | Ngo Mon gate, Thai Hoa palace, The Mieu temple, Forbidden Purple City, royal antiquities museum |
| **Citadel + 2 tombs** | 420,000 VND | Any two of Minh Mang, Tu Duc, Khai Dinh |
| **Citadel + 3 tombs** | 530,000 VND | All three of the above; the best value if you have two days |
| **All monuments** | 600,000 VND | Adds Gia Long, Dong Khanh, Nam Giao, An Dinh palace |
| **Single tomb** | 150,000 VND | Minh Mang, Tu Duc, Khai Dinh or Gia Long |

The citadel opens at 6:30 in summer and 7:00 in winter, closing at 18:00 and 17:30 respectively. Cash is still the safe assumption at the counters.

**Imperial City (Dai Noi).** Two to three hours, minimum. Enter through Ngo Mon, cross the bridge over the lotus pond, and work outward from Thai Hoa palace rather than trying to see everything. The restoration of Kien Trung palace has added a genuinely interesting stop; the Forbidden Purple City itself is largely a garden of foundations, which is its own kind of history.

**Khai Dinh tomb.** The strangest of the tombs: a concrete-and-mosaic hybrid of Vietnamese and European architecture, covered in dragon mosaics, with a bronze statue of the emperor under a gilded canopy. Steep stairs, best light mid-morning.

**Tu Duc tomb.** The most livable of them: pine trees, a lake, pavilions where the emperor composed poetry and, by most accounts, never slept. Allow 1.5 hours and sit down somewhere.

**Minh Mang tomb.** The most formally perfect, symmetrical layout, half a day if you include the boat.

**Thien Mu pagoda.** Seven storeys on a hill above the river, free to enter, and the source of the most famous photograph of Hue — the Austin car of the monk Thich Quang Duc is kept behind the main hall. Combine it with a dragon boat downstream.

**Dong Ba market.** Not a museum piece: a working market where you can buy conical hats, sesame candy and the ingredients for a proper bun bo. Go early.

**If you have a third day**: Thanh Toan tile bridge in the countryside, the Tiger Arena, and the abandoned water park at Thuy Tien lake — the last one is technically closed, and travellers visit at their own discretion.

## A two-day route that respects the heat

**Day 1 — the citadel, then the river.** Citadel at 7:30, out by 11:00 before the heat peaks. Lunch, then a long café break — Hue's café culture is a legitimate attraction. Late afternoon: Tu Duc tomb, when the light comes through the pines. Evening: dragon boat on the Perfume River, or a ca Hue folk-song performance.

**Day 2 — tombs and pagoda.** Khai Dinh at 8:00 (it is small but exposed), Minh Mang at 10:00, then a boat back along the river to Thien Mu. Late lunch of bun bo Hue, then Dong Ba market and an early dinner.

**Day 3 — countryside.** Cycle to Thanh Toan bridge, lunch in a garden house, and a cooking class in the afternoon. This is the day that explains Hue rather than photographing it.

## Where to stay

| Area | Feel | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **South bank, around Pham Ngu Lao** | Backpacker and mid-range, restaurants, walkable | Budget, solo travellers, food |
| **Riverside, Le Loi / Nguyen Hue** | Boutique hotels with river views | Couples, short stays |
| **Citadel side (north bank)** | Quieter, local, close to the monuments | Repeat visitors, photographers |
| **Thuan An beach** | 15 km out, resort-style | Beach plus city combination |

Most people are happiest on the south bank: the restaurants are better, the river walk is pleasant at night, and the citadel is a 10-minute ride across the bridge.

## What to eat in Hue

Hue's food is the most refined in Vietnam, built on small portions and many dishes.

- **Bun bo Hue** — the beef noodle soup the world knows by the city's name. In Hue it is cleaner and more chilli-forward than the southern versions, with pork knuckle and congealed blood. Breakfast.
- **Com hen** — baby mussel rice with peanuts, herbs and a sour broth poured over. Mix, then eat.
- **Banh beo, banh loc, banh nam** — the trio of steamed rice cakes, sold in sets of eight on a single plate.
- **Nem lui** — lemongrass pork skewers you grill at the table and wrap yourself.
- **Che** — the sweet soups; Hue's version is more delicate than Saigon's.
- **Ca phe muoi** — salted coffee, which Hue claims as its own and now sells on every second corner.
- **Royal cuisine** — the multi-course imperial set is expensive and uneven; book a reputable house rather than a tourist restaurant.

A practical note: portions are small by design. Two or three dishes per person is normal, and eating four is not greed — it is the intended experience.

## Practical notes

- **Rain**: buy a proper rain jacket, not an umbrella. Hue rain comes sideways.
- **Flooding**: October and November can flood the low streets near the river. Hotels move guests; transport gets slow. Build a spare day.
- **Transport**: bicycle rental is the best way to see tombs outside the centre; Grab works well inside the city; cyclos are for the experience, not for speed.
- **Dress**: shoulders and knees covered at pagodas and inside the citadel's ceremonial halls.
- **Trains**: the Hue–Da Nang leg over the Hai Van Pass is one of the most scenic short rail journeys in the country. Sit on the left heading south.
- **Money**: cash for monuments, markets and small restaurants. Card acceptance is improving but patchy.

## Festival note

Hue runs its festival year in four seasons — spring, summer, autumn and winter — with the 2026 edition opening on 1 January at Ngo Mon gate and closing with a countdown on 31 December. Most street events, parades and boat races are free; only grandstand seats and monument entry are ticketed. Check the exact programme close to your dates, because the seasonal schedules are published late.

## FAQ

**Is two days enough for Hue?**
Enough for the citadel, two tombs, the pagoda and a proper meal. Three days is where the city starts to make sense.

**Should I buy the combo ticket?**
If you plan to see the citadel plus two tombs, yes — 420,000 VND instead of 500,000 VND, and the extra day removes the pressure.

**What if it rains the whole time?**
Hue is the best city in Vietnam to be rained in: cafés, cooking classes, the royal antiquities museum, the Ho Chi Minh museum, and long lunches. Keep one indoor day in reserve.

**Which tomb should I skip if I only have time for one?**
Khai Dinh for impact and weirdness, Tu Duc for atmosphere. Skip Minh Mang if you are short on time and heat tolerance.

**Can I visit Hue as a day trip from Da Nang?**
Yes, and many people do — but it is a 2.5-hour train each way, which leaves five tired hours in the middle. Two nights is better.

**Is Hue good for families?**
Yes: short distances, the dragon boat, the citadel's open spaces and non-spicy food options. Avoid the hottest months.
