# Con Dao: an island that holds two opposite things at once

On the same island, less than twenty minutes apart by scooter, there is a cemetery with thousands of graves and a white beach with no footprints on it. Con Dao does not try to soften that contradiction. It is exactly why people remember this island longer than they remember prettier beaches.

This Con Dao travel guide sets out what you actually need before booking: which months work, how to get there, what things cost, and the rules of behaviour that visitors are rarely told.

## Where Con Dao is — and which province it belongs to now

The archipelago has 16 islands and about 76 km² of land, of which Con Son is the largest at just over 51 km². The highest point is Thanh Gia mountain at 577 m.

> **Administrative update (2026):** on 1 July 2025 Ba Ria–Vung Tau province was merged into Ho Chi Minh City. Con Dao is now the **Con Dao special administrative zone (dac khu Con Dao), Ho Chi Minh City**. Older guides still say "Con Dao, Ba Ria–Vung Tau" — that is no longer correct.

Con Dao National Park was established in 1993 and protects roughly 6,000 ha of land and 14,000 ha of water across 14 islands. It holds Vietnam's largest sea turtle population, and it is the only place in the country where a dugong population lives permanently attached to seagrass meadows.

## When to go

| Window | Conditions | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **December – April** | Dry, calm sea, best visibility, 24–31°C | Best weather, highest prices |
| **May – August** | 26–31°C, squalls, turtles nesting | Rougher crossings, but this is turtle season |
| **September – November** | Heaviest rain, strong wind, ferries cancelled | Cheapest rates, highest schedule risk |

**Choose by purpose:**

- Diving and clear water: **March to May**.
- Seeing a turtle nest: **June to August**, booked well ahead.
- Cheap and flexible about weather: **September to October**, while accepting the risk of a cancelled crossing.

Avoid late September to November if your dates are fixed. The northeast monsoon arrives early, fast ferries stop, and flights get reshuffled.

## Getting there

**By air:** about one hour from Ho Chi Minh City. Con Dao airport reopened in 2024 after an upgrade with a longer runway. Seats sell out weeks ahead in high season — book three to six weeks out.

**By fast ferry:** the Vung Tau crossing is 97 nautical miles and takes about 3 hours 15 minutes by fast boat. There are also services from Tran De (Soc Trang) and Can Tho. The traditional slow ship takes around 12 hours and is only worth it if the crossing itself is the point.

**The rule that saves trips:** book the flight first, then build the itinerary. If you are flying home to an international connection from Saigon, leave at least one buffer night. Wind cancels small aircraft here fairly often, and one rescheduled flight cascades through everything.

## What to see

### The Con Dao prison complex

The prison system was established by the French on 1 February 1862 and later expanded by the Republic of Vietnam into a detention and exile complex, best known for its tiger cages. An estimated 20,000 people died within the system and were buried at Hang Duong cemetery.

Remaining camps include Phu Hai, Phu Son, Phu Duong and the tiger cage area. Start at the museum, then walk the camps — that order makes what you are looking at legible.

- Go between 7 and 9 am; there is almost no shade on the site.
- Dress modestly: shoulders covered.
- This is a national memorial. Keep your voice low, and do not pose smiling in front of the tiger cages.

### Hang Duong cemetery

The resting place of thousands of prisoners, including Vo Thi Sau. Locals and visitors come to burn incense, especially in the evening. There is no ticket; keep quiet and do not film up close while people are performing rituals.

### Dam Trau beach

A curved beach beside the airport, backed by casuarina trees. Best around 4 pm, when the light slants and the crowd thins. Past the tree line there is no shade at all.

### Nhat beach

A long west-facing beach with a shallow entry, good for walking and an afternoon swim. There are almost no facilities, so bring water and a snack.

### Bay Canh island and turtle tours

The main green turtle nesting beach, with a ranger station and a hatchery. Access requires a permit and you go with rangers.

Non-negotiable rules: do not touch the turtles, no flash photography, never block the path to the sea, and stay behind the line the rangers set. Nesting runs roughly May to October, peaking June to August, and nightly places are strictly limited.

### Diving

Hon Ba and Hon Cau have coral and reef fish, with occasional dugong sightings over the seagrass. Visibility peaks February to May. Book with a licensed shop in town and ask when their equipment was last serviced.

## A three-day plan

| Day | Morning | Afternoon | Evening |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Fly or ferry in, check in, hire a scooter | Con Dao Museum, then the prison complex | Seafood at Con Son market, walk the waterfront |
| **Day 2** | Dam Trau 7–9 am | Nhat beach, or a two-tank dive | Hang Duong cemetery at dusk |
| **Day 3** | Bay Canh turtle tour or a national park trail | Ong Dung beach, last swim | Evening flight, or a fourth night as buffer |

Treat day three as a buffer day. If the flight is cancelled, you still have a window to rearrange instead of losing the return leg entirely.

## Practical notes

- **Cash is close to mandatory.** A handful of ATMs, often empty. Bring 3–5 million dong from the mainland in mixed denominations.
- **A scooter is the main transport.** 150,000–250,000 VND per day. No ride-hailing, very few taxis. The roads are empty and genuinely enjoyable — but watch for free-roaming dogs and sand on the bends.
- **National park permits:** Bay Canh and some trails require registration at park headquarters in town. Be sceptical of any agent promising access without paperwork.
- **Turtle ethics:** rangers control the beach tightly. Follow instructions even if it means standing still for thirty minutes.
- **Phone signal:** fine in town, weak at the far beaches. Download offline maps before you cross.
- **Sunscreen:** reef-safe only. The seagrass and coral here are strictly protected.

## FAQ

**How many days do I need?**
Three is the minimum to cover both the memorial sites and the beaches. Four lets you add Bay Canh or a dive without rushing.

**Ferry or flight?**
Flying is far quicker (about 1 hour versus 3h15) but sells out and cancels in wind. The ferry is cheaper and more predictable, though the swell can be rough.

**Can I see nesting turtles independently?**
No. It requires a national park permit and a guided visit, with a nightly cap on numbers.

**Is it suitable for children?**
Yes, but the prison complex can be distressing for young children. Think about age before entering the tiger cage area.

**Is Con Dao good for a pure beach holiday?**
Yes, if you pick a resort at Dam Trau or Nhat beach. But do not skip the history — it is half the reason this island matters to Vietnamese people.
