# Da Lat in 2026: a cool plateau with waterfalls, pine roads and serious coffee

Da Lat sits at roughly 1,500 m, which keeps temperatures between 15 and 24°C most of the year and drops them to 10–15°C at night. That alone makes the city unlike anywhere else in Vietnam. But the plateau is more than a place to escape the heat: there are waterfalls to descend on ropes, pine forest roads to cycle, and the country's most convincing arabica country.

The hard part is telling the genuinely good stops from the ones that only look good online. This Da Lat travel guide sticks to places worth the time, current prices, and a three-day plan that does not turn into a day of driving.

## What is worth doing

Four clusters. In town, Crazy House, Xuan Huong Lake and the night market. North, Lang Biang mountain and the coffee farms around Cau Dat. South, Datanla falls with its alpine coaster and canyoning. East, Truc Lam monastery above Tuyen Lam Lake.

Da Lat suits slow travel: coffee in the morning, a short ride, a hillside in the afternoon mist. If your idea of a good trip is eight stops a day, this city will resist you — the mountain roads and the weather see to that.

## When to go

| Period | Conditions | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| November–March | Dry, cool, clear skies | Walking, cycling, photography |
| December–February | Chilly mornings, flower season | Flowers, slow weekends |
| April–May | Mild, short showers, strawberry harvest | Fruit farms, waterfalls |
| June–October | Frequent rain, full waterfalls, deep green | Canyoning, waterfall days |

The rainy season here usually means afternoon or evening downpours with bright mornings. For canyoning, that is actually the best water, provided you go with a licensed operator and check releases.

December and January are both the flower season and the busiest stretch. Room rates jump at weekends and during holidays; booking three to four weeks ahead makes a real difference.

## Crazy House and the town centre

Crazy House (Hang Nga Guesthouse) was designed by architect Dang Viet Nga from 1990: a cluster of tree-like structures with almost no right angles, connected by narrow bridges. Entry is 80,000 VND and it is open 08:30–18:00. Eleven themed rooms can be booked for around 1.0–2.7 million VND a night.

It gets crowded between 09:00 and 15:00. Arrive at opening or after 16:00 for space to photograph. Inside, the staircases are narrow and the railings low, which is not ideal for anyone afraid of heights or for very small children.

Xuan Huong Lake is the centre's anchor. The loop is about 5 km, 45–90 minutes on foot or by bike, with coffee shops along the shore. Start here in the morning before the paid attractions open.

## Lang Biang: the jeep does not go to the summit

This is the most common misunderstanding in Da Lat. The Lang Biang tourist area runs jeeps to **Radar Hill**, about 1,950 m, where you get the panorama over Dan Kia Lake and the pine forest. **Lady Peak**, the highest summit of the range at 2,167 m, is reached on foot only.

Entry is 50,000 VND; the return jeep to Radar Hill is 120,000 VND per person, departing when six seats fill. Since late 2024, the informal hiking trail to the summit has been suspended for safety reasons; trekking now requires a licensed operator and a check on trail status before you go.

With a single morning, the jeep up and down is enough. Just do not buy the jeep ticket expecting to stand on the highest point.

## Datanla: coaster, zipline and canyoning

Datanla sits about 5 km south of town on the Prenn Pass and holds most of Da Lat's adventure activities.

Prices as of October 2026:

| Activity | Adult price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Waterfall entry | 80,000 VND | Walk down to the falls |
| Alpine coaster line 1, return | 130,000 VND | Entry ticket not included |
| Alpine coaster line 3, return | 250,000 VND | Includes entry, longer track |
| Zipline 1,500 m | 1,000,000 VND | Sales 08:00–15:30 |
| Canyoning | about 1,200,000–1,900,000 VND | 5–6 hours with guides |

Canyoning is the best value here if you are fit: abseiling down rock walls, ziplining over a waterfall, swimming through cold pools. The site's own operator sells it at around 1.9 million VND. Outside operators can be cheaper, but ask whether insurance is included, whether they use the site's equipment and whether they are permitted to operate inside the park.

Never descend the falls independently when the water is high. In the rainy season, flow can rise within hours.

## Cau Dat coffee and the countryside

Cau Dat lies about 25 km southeast of the city, at 1,500–1,600 m, and has been growing arabica for generations. The road passes tea and coffee hills, beautiful but steep and narrow; check your brakes before committing on a motorbike.

Several farms take visitors through the processing steps and offer tastings. Expect 100,000–250,000 VND per person depending on whether drinks are included. Weasel coffee, processed through civet digestion, costs many times more than ordinary arabica; if you buy, take whole beans and have them ground on site.

East of town, Truc Lam Monastery sits on a hill above Tuyen Lam Lake. The cable car from Robin Hill runs about 2.3 km and costs roughly 100,000–150,000 VND return, taking 10–15 minutes. The monastery welcomes visitors, but dress modestly and keep quiet.

## A three-day plan

| Day | Morning | Afternoon | Evening |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Crazy House at 08:30, walk Xuan Huong Lake | Datanla falls, coaster line 3 | Night market, grilled corn, hot soy milk |
| 2 | Lang Biang by jeep to Radar Hill | Cau Dat coffee farms and tasting | Coffee in town, night market |
| 3 | Canyoning at Datanla, or Truc Lam by cable car | Strawberry farm, Da Lat–Trai Mat train | Depart, or stay another night |

With children or older travellers, replace canyoning with the cable car and a flower garden. If adventure is the point of the trip, give day three entirely to canyoning and drop the town stops.

The night market on Nguyen Thi Minh Khai runs from about 17:00 to 22:00. It is for snacking, not for dinner; the hotpot and rice shops in the side streets nearby serve better food for similar money.

## Getting around and practical notes

Lien Khuong airport is about 30 km from the centre; taxis run 250,000–350,000 VND. Grab and Be exist in town but coverage thins quickly outside it.

A rented motorbike at 100,000–150,000 VND per day is the most flexible option, but mountain roads have blind corners and morning fog. Carry a valid licence and wear a helmet.

Three practical points:

- Pack a jacket for evenings even in April. Day-to-night temperature swings of 10°C are normal.
- Many breakfast places close by 10:00 and run out of food earlier.
- Country roads are narrow. For groups, a hired seven-seater with a driver is often cheaper than three or four motorbikes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Da Lat cold?**
Cool rather than cold. Average 15–24°C, nights 10–15°C. A light jacket covers the day; evenings need something warmer.

**How many days do I need?**
Three days covers the main sights. Four if you want Cau Dat, Bidoup National Park or more rest.

**Can I do canyoning if I cannot swim?**
No. The activity requires swimming and comfort in water; guides assess participants before the descent.

**Can I reach the Lang Biang summit by jeep?**
No. Jeeps stop at Radar Hill around 1,950 m. The 2,167 m summit is a guided hike.

**Where should I stay?**
Central Da Lat near Xuan Huong Lake is best for walking and food. Tuyen Lam Lake suits quiet resort stays. Cau Dat suits coffee lovers who do not mind being 25 km out.
