# Hanoi: A City Best Read at Walking Speed

Hanoi is not a checklist. The Opera House, the lake, the mausoleum — you will see them all in two days if you hurry, and you will remember almost none of it. The city opens differently: through a bowl of broth at seven in the morning, a chair too low for the table, a shop selling only silver on a street that has sold only silver for centuries.

This Hanoi travel guide is built for that version of the city. It covers what is verified, what has changed recently, and what is often repeated online but no longer true.

## Why Hanoi still rewards the curious

Four things separate Hanoi from every other stop on a Vietnam itinerary.

**Layered streets.** The Old Quarter's guild logic survives in its street names — Hàng Bạc for silver, Hàng Gai for silk, Hàng Mã for paper offerings. The guilds themselves are gone, but the trade clusters are not. You can still walk one block and change industries.

**A cafe culture that predates the tourist boom.** Coffee here is a habit, not an attraction. Sidewalk stalls, second-floor hideaways, plastic stools at dawn — the city conducts a good part of its social life over a small cup.

**A political core you can walk through.** Ba Dinh Square, the mausoleum, the One Pillar Pagoda, the Presidential Palace and Ho Chi Minh's stilt house sit within a few hundred metres of each other. Nowhere else in Southeast Asia can you cover that much modern history on foot before lunch.

**Real countryside within two hours.** Ninh Binh's limestone valleys, Ha Long's water, and craft villages like Bát Tràng and Vạn Phúc are all day-trip distance. Hanoi works as a base, not just a stop.

## When to go

| Season | Months | What it feels like | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autumn | Sep–Nov | Cool, dry, soft golden light; milk-flower (hoa sữa) scent in October | Best window overall |
| Spring | Mar–Apr | Warming, humid, occasional drizzle | Good, but pack layers |
| Summer | May–Aug | 33–38°C, heavy afternoon showers, intense sun | Do mornings and evenings, hide at midday |
| Winter | Dec–Feb | 10–16°C, grey drizzle, thin mist | Atmospheric and quiet, but cold and damp |

Two dates matter more than the weather. **Tết** (Lunar New Year, late January or February) shuts down small businesses for three to five days and pushes transport prices up — an interesting but logistically awkward time. And the mausoleum closes for roughly two months each autumn for preservation work, so check before you plan a trip around it.

## A three-day route that holds up

### Day 1 — Old Quarter, on foot

Start early, before the heat and the tour groups. A bowl of phở at 6:30 is not a cliché; it is how the neighbourhood wakes up. Walk the trade streets, then cut through the market alleys behind them.

Late morning: **Bún chả** — grilled pork patties, cold vermicelli, herbs, warm dipping broth. It is a lunch dish; many stalls sell out by 2 pm and close. **Bún thăng**, a delicate chicken-and-egg noodle soup, is the quieter Hanoi specialty and rarely appears on English menus.

Afternoon: **St. Joseph's Cathedral**'s neo-Gothic facade, then **Hỏa Lò Prison**, whose exhibits present the French colonial period and the American war from a Vietnamese perspective. Give it an hour and an open mind.

Evening: egg coffee, then the weekend night market on Hàng Đào–Hàng Giấy if your dates line up.

### Day 2 — Ba Dinh, temples, puppets

The mausoleum is a morning-only visit, closed Monday and Friday, and queues build fast. Arriving by 7:00–7:15 is worth the lost hour of sleep. Dress code is enforced: shoulders and knees covered, hats off, no photography inside, bags checked.

Then walk the complex — the One Pillar Pagoda, the Presidential Palace, the stilt house — before the sun gets serious. In the afternoon, the **Temple of Literature**, founded in 1070 and widely described as Vietnam's first university, is at its best in low light; the stone stelae of doctorate holders are the detail most visitors miss.

Evening: the **Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre** near the lake. Shows run about 50 minutes, several times daily from mid-afternoon to around 8 pm, tickets roughly 100,000–200,000 VND depending on seat tier. Book a day ahead at weekends. Afterward, **chả cá** — turmeric-marinated fish seared at the table with dill — at one of the two long-established addresses on Chả Cá street.

### Day 3 — pick one direction

- **Ninh Bình, 2 hours south:** Tràng An's boat caves, the Mua Cave viewpoint (roughly 500 steps), Tam Cốc's rice paddies. The strongest day trip.
- **Ha Long Bay, 2.5 hours east:** Only worth it as an overnight. A day trip spends more time on the road than on the water.
- **Craft villages:** Bát Tràng ceramics (45 minutes), Vạn Phúc silk, Đường Lâm's laterite lanes (about an hour).
- **A guided food walk:** Seven or eight stops, one market, and someone who can order for you. Best value if you only have one evening free.

Note on phở: in Hanoi it is beef, and the broth is meant to be clear. Chicken phở belongs to the south. Ordering it in the north is not a crime, only a sign you are in the wrong conversation.

## Egg coffee: the short version

In 1946, a bartender named Nguyễn Văn Giảng worked at the Metropole, then as now Hanoi's grand colonial hotel. Milk was scarce and expensive in the turmoil after the war, so he whipped egg yolk with sweetened condensed milk and poured strong coffee underneath it. The result behaved like a dessert and drank like a wake-up call.

He left the hotel and opened **Cafe Giang** at 39 Nguyễn Hữu Huân, still run by his family, still using the original approach. His daughter runs **Cafe Dinh** at 13 Đinh Tiên Hoàng, a second-floor room overlooking the lake — arguably the better view, and a quieter cup. Both are worth your time; the recipe is a family argument, not a secret formula.

## Train Street: what the rules actually mean now

The railway through the Old Quarter is a working line, and the strip of cafes alongside it has been contested for years. The current situation, as of late 2026:

- Cafes along the track operate, but access is through those licensed businesses, not the street itself.
- **Organised group tours have been banned since March 2025**, and barriers with guards appear at the main entrances.
- There is no official entry ticket; you are a customer of a cafe, not a visitor to an attraction.
- Trains pass only a few times a day, roughly every few hours — cafe staff know the day's schedule and will tell you.
- Climbing barriers or slipping in through private houses is both unsafe and a genuine nuisance to residents.

My honest read: if you want the experience, go to a licensed cafe, order something, wait for the train, and leave when it has passed. It is a fifteen-minute moment that photographs beautifully. It is not worth an argument with a guard or a fine.

## Practical details that save a day

**Crossing the road.** Walk at a steady pace, in a straight line, without sudden stops. Drivers calculate around predictable movement. Running or freezing is what creates accidents.

**Airport to the centre.** Express **bus 86** runs from Noi Bai to Long Biên and Hanoi Railway Station, roughly 45,000 VND, about an hour, from early morning until around 10 pm. Slower local buses (07, 17) cost about 9,000 VND but take longer and are crowded. Grab or Be runs 250,000–350,000 VND. Bus 86 is the best value if your luggage is manageable.

**Mausoleum, again.** Tuesday to Sunday mornings only. April–October 7:30–10:30; November–March 8:00–11:00. Closed Monday and Friday, closed every afternoon, and closed for annual maintenance in autumn.

**Cash.** Small vendors, market stalls and many cafes are cash-only. Carry 20,000 / 50,000 / 100,000 notes. ATMs are everywhere but foreign-card fees add up.

**Water puppets.** 57B Đinh Tiên Hoàng, a minute from the lake. Buy at the box office or the official site; touts charge a markup for the same seat.

**Vegetarian.** Say *chay*. Every phở shop understands it; dedicated vegetarian restaurants cluster around the Old Quarter and Tay Ho.

## FAQ

### How many days do I need in Hanoi?
Three full days covers the city properly and allows one day trip. Two days works if you skip Ninh Bình; five days is the point where Hanoi stops being a destination and starts being a place you know.

### Is Hanoi safe?
Violent crime against visitors is rare. The realistic risks are traffic, bag-snatching from motorbikes near the lake at night, and drink-price games on Bia Hoi corner. Keep your phone out of your hand near the road.

### Is Train Street worth it?
Yes, once, if you accept the current rules: enter through a licensed cafe, buy a drink, wait for a scheduled train, and do not climb barriers. If crowds bother you, skip it — the same railway passes through ordinary neighbourhoods you can watch from a bridge.

### Can I visit the mausoleum in the afternoon?
No. Mornings only, Tuesday to Sunday, and it closes entirely on Monday and Friday plus roughly two months each autumn.

### How do I get to Ha Long Bay?
Two and a half to three hours by road to Tuần Châu marina, usually included in overnight cruise packages. Do not attempt a same-day return from Hanoi; the bay deserves a night.
