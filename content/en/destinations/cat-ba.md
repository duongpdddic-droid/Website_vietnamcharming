# Cat Ba in 2026: Lan Ha Bay without the Ha Long armada

Of the 367 islands scattered south of Ha Long Bay, Cat Ba is the biggest, and about half of it is protected park. The limestone towers are the same ones that draw the crowds further north; the difference is the water around them, which stays quiet. Lan Ha Bay, on the island's south side, holds more than 400 islets, a handful of floating villages and channels flat enough to kayak through cave arches.

What makes Cat Ba different is the combination. You can spend a morning on the bay, an afternoon climbing through forest, and an evening in a town that still runs on fishing schedules. This Cat Ba travel guide covers the ferry crossing, the Lan Ha Bay boat, the national park trek and where to sleep, in the order that keeps the three days from collapsing.

## What to do on Cat Ba

Lan Ha Bay is the main event: kayaking, Van Boi cave, floating villages and small beaches. On the island, Cat Ba National Park covers 17,362 hectares including its marine section, with trails up Ngu Lam peak at 225 m. Hospital Cave, a three-storey wartime hospital built inside a mountain between 1963 and 1965, sits along the road.

The island is also the only place on earth with the Cat Ba langur, a critically endangered primate subspecies. Fewer than 100 remain; recent surveys put the population at roughly 79–90. Sightings are rare, but the animal is why this park matters internationally.

In 2023, the Cat Ba Archipelago was inscribed as an extension of the Ha Long Bay World Heritage site. The area has been a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve since 2004.

## When to go

| Period | Conditions | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| April–June | 24–32°C, calm sea, clear water | Best for kayaking and climbing |
| July–August | Hot, peak domestic season, prices double | Book rooms and boats early |
| September–November | 22–29°C, seas settle after typhoon season | Fewer crowds, good light |
| December–March | 15–22°C, mist, choppy sea | Cheapest, some boats suspended |

July and August are when Vietnamese families travel; the piers are busy and room rates jump. Typhoons are possible in August and September, and ferries stop in high wind. If you have a flight after Cat Ba, keep a buffer day.

## Getting there from Hanoi

| Route | Time | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Tourist bus + ferry from the Old Quarter | 3.5–4 hours | 300,000–450,000 VND |
| Independent: bus to Hai Phong, local bus to Dong Bai pier, ferry to Cai Vieng | 4–5 hours | around 200,000 VND |
| Speedboat from Ben Binh, Hai Phong | 45 minutes | around 250,000 VND |
| Fly to Cat Bi, taxi to the pier, ferry | about 3 hours from landing | 1–1.7 million VND |

The Dong Bai–Cai Vieng ferry takes about 30 minutes, runs roughly 05:00–19:30 in high season at 30–60 minute intervals. Foot passengers pay about 12,000 VND, motorbikes 45,000 VND, cars under nine seats 190,000 VND. Cai Vieng is about 16 km from Cat Ba town.

Note that the pier name has changed from older guides: Ben Got is no longer the main crossing. Ferries now leave from Dong Bai on Cat Hai island.

## Lan Ha Bay: choosing a boat

Day trips usually run 08:00–16:30, passing karst islets, stopping to kayak around Van Boi or the Bright–Dark caves, with lunch on board and a swim stop. Expect 700,000–1,200,000 VND per person depending on boat size and food. Overnight cruises run 2.3–5 million VND, with cabin quality driving the difference.

Ask before booking: how many guests on board, whether kayaking is actually included or replaced by a rowing boat, what lunch consists of, and whether the bay entrance fee is extra. The Lan Ha Bay admission fee is about 80,000 VND.

Kayaking here is easier than most people expect because the water is flat, but some arches require ducking low. Bring a dry bag and a rash guard; reflection off the water burns faster than a day at the beach.

## The national park and Ngu Lam peak

Park entry is 80,000 VND, cash only. The Ngu Lam trail starts near Trung Trang, climbs for 1–1.5 hours to a 225 m summit, and opens a 360° view over the island and bay. Start at 06:30 to beat the heat and catch birds and macaques.

The rock is polished and slippery after rain; grippy shoes matter. There are few water vendors on the trail, so carry 1–1.5 litres.

**Hospital Cave** sits about 7 km from the park centre: three floors, around 17 rooms, capacity close to 100 beds, built inside the mountain between 1963 and 1965. Tickets run about 40,000–80,000 VND depending on the period; bring a torch for the lower chambers.

**Cannon Fort** occupies a 177 m hill south of town, built by the French from 1942, with tunnels and two large guns. It is the best sunset viewpoint on the island. Entry is around 50,000–80,000 VND.

## A three-day itinerary

| Day | Morning | Afternoon | Evening |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Leave Hanoi at 07:00, ferry from Dong Bai, in town by 11:00 | Cat Co 1–2 walk and swim | Seafood on the waterfront, book a bay tour |
| 2 | Lan Ha Bay boat at 08:00, kayaking, Van Boi cave | Swim stop, Monkey Island on the way back | Cannon Fort sunset, night market |
| 3 | Ngu Lam at 06:30, then Hospital Cave | Cycle Viet Hai village or visit Trung Trang Cave | Ferry back to Hanoi |

With two days, drop Ngu Lam and give the second day to Lan Ha. With small children, swap the trek for cycling in Viet Hai and a swim at Cat Co 3.

One ethical note: horseshoe crab is a protected species. A menu offering it is a red flag, not a bargain.

## Getting around and practical notes

Scooter rental runs 120,000–180,000 VND per day. Roads are narrow with blind corners; helmets are mandatory but often flimsy, and you should test the brakes before riding off.

The Sun World Cat Ba cable car connects the mainland to the island, with return tickets around 200,000 VND — an alternative to the ferry if you are not bringing a vehicle.

Three practical points:

- There are only a couple of ATMs in town and they fail often. Bring cash from Hanoi.
- Power cuts still happen; carry a power bank.
- Plastic waste is a visible problem. Bring a refillable bottle.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many days do I need on Cat Ba?**
Three days and two nights covers Lan Ha Bay, Ngu Lam and one historical site. Two days means choosing either the bay or the park.

**Is Cat Ba better than Ha Long?**
If you want the same limestone scenery with fewer boats and fewer groups, yes. If you want high-end cruise variety, Ha Long still offers more.

**Is Cat Ba easy to do independently?**
Yes, but you must change transport in Hai Phong and reach Dong Bai pier. A through bus from Hanoi is easier with luggage.

**Will I see the Cat Ba langur?**
Unlikely. They live on steep cliffs and avoid people. An early morning walk with a park guide is the best chance, but treat a sighting as a bonus.

**Can children climb Ngu Lam?**
Yes, if they are over about eight and wearing grippy shoes. The trail is short but has steep rock sections.

## Plan Cat Ba around the ferry, not just the hotel

Cat Ba depends on ferries and weather more than most destinations. A good trip starts with picking the right dates, booking the bay boat early and keeping a spare half-day before any flight. We can sequence two or three days including the crossing, the Lan Ha tour and the trek, and check the actual ferry schedule for your dates so you are not stuck at a pier.
