# Hoan Kiem Lake: The Heart That Hanoi Beats Around

Hoan Kiem Lake is not the biggest lake in Hanoi, nor the most scenic. It is the one the city orbits. Every morning, hundreds of locals practice tai chi, jog, or simply sit on the benches facing the water. Every evening, couples walk the lit path and families push strollers along the shaded banks. The lake is 12 hectares of calm in a city of 8 million.

This guide covers the legend, the temple, the tower, the best times to visit, and the small details that make the lake more than a photo stop.

## Location & getting there

The lake sits in the center of Hanoi's Hoan Kiem District, 200 m south of the Old Quarter and 1 km north of the French Quarter.

**From airport:** Bus 86 to Hoan Kiem (last stop), 45,000 VND, 50–60 min. Or taxi/Grab 300,000–450,000 VND.

**From train station:** 2 km north. Grab 40,000–60,000 VND or 20-min walk up Pho Hue.

**From within city:** Walk from any central hotel. The lake is the reference point for all Hanoi navigation.

## Highlights not to miss

- **The Legend:** In the 15th century, Emperor Le Loi received a magical sword from the Golden Turtle God to drive out Chinese invaders. After victory, he returned the sword to the lake — hence "Hoan Kiem" (Lake of the Returned Sword). The story is carved into the temple and told to every Vietnamese schoolchild.
- **Ngoc Son Temple (Temple of the Jade Mountain):** Built in the 19th century on a small island. Reached by the iconic red **The Huc Bridge** (Bridge of the Rising Sun). Houses altars to Tran Hung Dao (military hero) and Van Xuong (literature deity).
- **Turtle Tower (Thap Rua):** A small tower on an islet in the lake's center. Symbol of Hanoi. Not open to visitors but visible from all sides. Best photographed from the east bank at sunset.
- **Morning tai chi:** 05:30–07:00, especially on the north and east banks. Locals gather in groups; visitors are welcome to watch or join quietly.
- **Turtle sightings:** The lake once held a giant softshell turtle (Rafetus swinhoei), last seen in 2016. A preserved specimen is in Ngoc Son Temple. Locals still scan the water hoping for a return.

## Suggested visit plan (1–2 hours)

1. **05:30–07:00:** Walk the full loop. Watch tai chi groups. Best light for photos.
2. **07:00–08:00:** Coffee at a lakeside cafe (Cong Caphe or Highland Coffee on Dinh Tien Hoang).
3. **08:00–09:00:** Cross The Huc Bridge to Ngoc Son Temple. 30,000 VND entry. 20–30 min visit.
4. **09:00–09:30:** Photo stop at Turtle Tower from east bank.
5. **Evening return:** 18:00–20:00 for sunset and lit paths. Street food vendors appear along the south bank.

**Tip:** The lake is most atmospheric at dawn. By 09:00 it's crowded with tour groups.

## Practical tips

- **Money:** Free lake; temple ticket 30,000 VND (cash). ATMs on Dinh Tien Hoang.
- **Dress:** Shoulders and knees covered for Ngoc Son Temple. Remove shoes at temple entrance.
- **Photos:** The Huc Bridge is most photogenic from the south bank in morning light. Turtle Tower from east bank at sunset.
- **Weekends:** Lake area closes to traffic Fri 19:00–Sun 24:00 — pedestrian-only zone. Great for families.
- **Heat:** May–Aug midday is brutal. Walk early or late.
- **Safety:** Well-lit and busy. Normal urban awareness sufficient.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I swim in Hoan Kiem Lake?**
No. Swimming is prohibited. The lake is a cultural and ecological site, not a recreational pool.

**Is Ngoc Son Temple worth the ticket?**
Yes, if you want the legend context and the red bridge photo. 20–30 minutes is enough.

**What time is best for photos?**
05:30–06:30 for empty paths and soft light. 17:30–18:30 for sunset behind Turtle Tower.

**Are there toilets near the lake?**
Public toilets at the south end (near Trang Tien street) and in cafes. 2,000–5,000 VND.

**Is the lake accessible for wheelchairs?**
The main path is flat and paved. Ngoc Son Temple has steps (no ramp). Bridge has steps.
