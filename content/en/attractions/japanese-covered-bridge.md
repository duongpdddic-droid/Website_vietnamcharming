# Japanese Covered Bridge: Hoi An's 400-Year-Old Heart

The Japanese Covered Bridge (Chua Cau) is the single most photographed structure in Hoi An. Built in 1593 by Japanese merchants, it is 18 m long, covered, and houses a small temple to the northern god (Bac De Tran Vo). It has been rebuilt several times — the last major restoration was in 1986 — but the original footprint and design remain.

The bridge sits at the west end of Tran Phu street, spanning a small canal that once connected the Japanese quarter to the Chinese quarter. Today it is the unofficial gateway to the Ancient Town.

## Location & getting there

The bridge is at the west end of Tran Phu street, where it meets Nguyen Thi Minh Khai street. It is the natural entry point to the Ancient Town from the west.

**From Hoi An bus station:** 2 km. Grab 30,000–50,000 VND or 15-min walk south.

**From Da Nang:** 30 km north. Taxi/Grab 300,000–450,000 VND, 45 min.

**From An Bang Beach:** 4 km east. Grab 60,000–80,000 VND, 10 min.

## Highlights not to miss

- **The roof:** Curved, tiled, with decorative carvings at the peaks. The roof style is distinctly Japanese — rare in Vietnam.
- **The temple:** Small shrine to Bac De Tran Vo (the northern god, protector against disasters). Locals still pray here. Remove shoes, speak quietly.
- **The monkey and dog statues:** At each end of the bridge. According to legend, construction started in the Year of the Monkey and finished in the Year of the Dog.
- **Night illumination:** After 18:00, the bridge is lit with lanterns and spotlights. The reflection in the canal is the classic Hoi An photo.
- **Canal view:** Look east from the bridge for the canal lined with old houses — the quintessential Hoi An street scene.

## Suggested visit plan (5–10 minutes)

1. **07:00–07:30:** Arrive before crowds. Photo from the east bank looking west at the bridge.
2. **07:30–07:45:** Cross the bridge. Enter the temple (ticket required). 5-min visit.
3. **07:45–08:00:** Photo from the west bank looking east — the bridge with the canal and old houses behind.
4. **Evening return:** 17:30–18:30 for golden hour + lanterns. The bridge is lit from 18:00.

**Tip:** The best photo is from the east bank (Tran Phu side) in the morning, or from a boat on the canal at sunset.

## Practical tips

- **Money:** Heritage ticket 120,000 VND (includes 5 sites). Buy at the ticket booth on Tran Phu or Nguyen Thi Minh Khai.
- **Crowds:** 09:00–17:00 is busy. The bridge is narrow — wait for gaps. Early morning and evening are best.
- **Photography:** No flash inside the temple. Tripods not allowed on the bridge (too narrow).
- **Dress:** Shoulders covered for temple entry. Shoes off inside.
- **Accessibility:** The bridge has steps at both ends. Not wheelchair accessible.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a ticket to cross the bridge?**
No. Walking across is free. The 120,000 VND heritage ticket is only needed to enter the temple inside.

**How long do I need?**
5–10 minutes for the bridge and temple. Add 15 min for photos from different angles.

**Is the bridge lit at night?**
Yes. Lanterns and spotlights from 18:00–22:00. The canal reflection is the classic night photo.

**Can I take photos inside the temple?**
Yes, but no flash. Remove shoes first.

**Is the bridge accessible for strollers?**
The bridge has steps at both ends. Strollers need to be carried. The canal path is flat.
