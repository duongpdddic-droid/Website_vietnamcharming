# Temple of Literature: Where Vietnam's First Scholars Were Made

The Temple of Literature (Van Mieu) is not a temple in the religious sense. It is a university campus — the first in Vietnam, founded in 1070 — where Confucian scholars studied, took examinations, and had their names carved in stone. The 82 stelae lining the courtyards list 1,306 doctoral graduates from 1442 to 1779. Each stela sits on a stone turtle, symbolizing longevity and wisdom.

This guide covers the history, the layout, the stelae, practical visiting details, and why this site matters more than its modest entrance suggests.

## Location & getting there

The Temple of Literature is 2 km west of Hoan Kiem Lake, on Quoc Tu Giam street in Dong Da District.

**From Hoan Kiem Lake:** 10-min Grab (40,000–60,000 VND) or 25-min walk west along Hang Bong → Quoc Tu Giam.

**From airport:** Bus 86 to Hoan Kiem, then Grab 10 min. Or taxi direct 350,000–500,000 VND.

**From train station:** 3 km. Grab 60,000–80,000 VND, 12 min.

## Highlights not to miss

- **Five Courtyards:** The complex is laid out in five sequential courtyards, moving from public space to scholarly space. Each courtyard has a gate with symbolic meaning.
- **Khue Van Cac (Constellation of Literature Pavilion):** The iconic two-tiered pavilion on the second courtyard. Built in 1805. Symbol of Hanoi — appears on the 100,000 VND banknote.
- **82 Doctor Stelae:** Lining the third courtyard. Each stela (1.5–2 m tall) sits on a stone turtle. The 1,306 names include the doctoral degree holder's name, birthplace, and examination year. UNESCO Memory of the World (2011).
- **Quoc Tu Giam (National Academy):** The fourth courtyard housed the university. The Thai Hoc house (rebuilt 2000) displays the history of Vietnamese education.
- **Dai Thanh Sanctuary:** The fifth courtyard, dedicated to Confucius and his disciples. Still used for ceremonies.

## Suggested visit plan (45–60 minutes)

1. **08:00–08:15:** Enter through Dai Tri gate. Walk through first courtyard (garden, pond).
2. **08:15–08:30:** Khue Van Cac pavilion — photo stop. The pavilion is best lit in morning.
3. **08:30–09:00:** Third courtyard — walk among the 82 stelae. Read a few names and provinces. Notice the turtle bases.
4. **09:00–09:20:** Quoc Tu Giam (fourth courtyard) — Thai Hoc house, education history exhibition.
5. **09:20–09:30:** Dai Thanh sanctuary (fifth courtyard) — incense, quiet.

**Tip:** Go at 08:00 on a weekday. By 10:00 tour groups fill the courtyards.

## Practical tips

- **Dress:** Shoulders and knees covered. Remove shoes at sanctuary entrances.
- **Photography:** No flash inside buildings. Tripods require permission.
- **Guides:** Available at gate (100,000–150,000 VND). Worth it for the stelae stories — each has a person behind the name.
- **Souvenirs:** Small shop near exit sells calligraphy, miniature stelae, and silk. Prices fixed.
- **Heat:** Courtyards have limited shade. May–Aug: visit early.
- **Accessibility:** Flat paths throughout. Wheelchair accessible except some building interiors.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the Temple of Literature worth visiting?**
Yes, especially if you care about history and education. The stelae are unique — no other site in Vietnam has this record of scholars.

**How long do I need?**
45–60 minutes for a self-guided walk. 90 minutes with a guide.

**Can I touch the stelae?**
No. The stelae are UNESCO-protected. Viewing only. Some have protective glass.

**Is there a dress code?**
Yes. Shoulders and knees covered. This is a cultural site, not a beach.

**What is the best time to visit?**
08:00–09:30 on a weekday. Cool, quiet, good light for the stelae.
