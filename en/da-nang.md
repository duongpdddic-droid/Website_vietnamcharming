# Da Nang: Beaches, Bridges and a Mountain Full of Caves

Da Nang is the rare Vietnamese city that does not need to perform. The beach runs for kilometres along the east side, a marble mountain full of caves sits at the southern end, a dragon-shaped bridge breathes fire over the Han River, and a 67-metre Lady Buddha watches all of it from a forested peninsula.

It is also easy to get wrong. People arrive in the wrong month, queue for the Golden Bridge at 11 a.m. in a crowd of coaches, and then decide the city is overrated. This Da Nang travel guide is about avoiding that.

## Da Nang at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Central coast, on the Han River; since July 2025 the city also administers Hoi An and My Son |
| **Airport** | Da Nang International (DAD), 3 km from the centre |
| **Best months** | February to May; September to October for value |
| **Beach season** | April to August for calm water and sun |
| **Minimum stay** | 3 nights, 4 if you add Hoi An |
| **Getting around** | Grab or Be; motorbike taxis are fastest; the centre is walkable |

## When to go

| Period | Weather | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Feb – Apr** | 24–30 °C, dry, clear mornings | The best all-round window; ideal for the Golden Bridge |
| **May – Aug** | 30–35 °C, humid, short evening storms | Great beach weather, heavy domestic tourism, book ahead |
| **Sep – Oct** | 26–31 °C, mixed sun and showers | Fewer crowds, better prices, occasional rough sea |
| **Nov – Dec** | 22–27 °C, rain spells, typhoon risk | Cheap and quiet; check forecasts if you planned boat trips |
| **Jan** | 20–25 °C, damp drizzle | Cool and grey; fine for food and city, not for beach days |

Two local rules of thumb: the sea is calmest from May to August, and the Golden Bridge is only worth photographing before 9 a.m. or after 4 p.m., when the light softens and the day-trippers thin out.

## The icons, with the details that matter

**Golden Bridge (Ba Na Hills).** A 150-metre pedestrian bridge held up by two enormous stone hands, sitting at 1,414 m above sea level on the Ba Na plateau. It opened in June 2018 and has been the city's most photographed object ever since. Getting there means a cable car ride of roughly 20 minutes; the bridge itself takes 30 minutes to walk and photograph.

**My Khe Beach.** About 9 km of fine sand running from the Son Tra peninsula toward Marble Mountains, once listed by Forbes among the world's most attractive beaches. Locals swim at dawn and again at sunset. The water is calmest in the morning; the sand is widest north of the main resort strip.

**Marble Mountains (Ngu Hanh Son).** Five limestone hills named after the five elements, of which only Thuy Son (Water) is open to visitors. Inside: Huyen Khong cave with a shaft of light at midday, Tam Thai and Linh Ung pagodas, and viewpoints over the coast. Entry is 40,000 VND; the lift is 15,000 VND each way and saves 108 steps, not the whole climb. Am Phu cave, at the base, is a separate ticket.

**Lady Buddha, Son Tra Peninsula.** A 67-metre statue of Guanyin, the tallest of its kind in Vietnam, completed in 2010 and free to visit. The base holds 17 floors of small altars; outside, the peninsula's 4,000 hectares of forest shelter the red-shanked douc langur, one of the world's most beautiful primates. Go at sunrise and keep a respectful distance from the monkeys.

**Dragon Bridge.** 666 metres of steel shaped like a dragon, crossing the Han River on six lanes. The head breathes fire for about two minutes, then water for three, at 21:00 on Friday, Saturday and Sunday, plus major holidays. The bridge closes to traffic around 20:45, so arrive by 20:30 and stand on the Bach Dang riverside rather than on the bridge itself.

**Cham Sculpture Museum.** The world's largest collection of Cham sandstone sculpture, in a French-colonial building beside the Han River. An hour here makes the My Son ruins three hours away far more legible.

## A three-day plan that does not exhaust you

**Day 1 — coast and river.** Swim at My Khe at 6 a.m., then Marble Mountains at 8 a.m. before the coaches arrive. Lunch, then Son Tra peninsula and the Lady Buddha in the afternoon. Evening: the dragon show at 21:00, then seafood on the beach side.

**Day 2 — Ba Na Hills, full day.** Leave at 8 a.m., ride up, walk the Golden Bridge first while it is still quiet, then the French Village, gardens and Fantasy Park. Come down by 16:00. Evening: a Han River cruise or a rooftop bar.

**Day 3 — slow morning.** Sunrise swim, Cham Museum at 10 a.m., then Han Market or Con Market for coffee, fish sauce and local snacks. If you have a fourth day, Hoi An's old town is 30 minutes south and best visited in the late afternoon.

## Where to stay

| Area | Feel | Good for | Typical range |
|---|---|---|---|
| **My Khe / Vo Nguyen Giap** | Resort strip facing the sea | Beach-first trips, families | 1.2–7 million VND |
| **Hai Chau (centre)** | Urban, walkable, near the river | First-timers, food, nightlife | 700,000–3.5 million VND |
| **Son Tra peninsula** | Quiet, green, luxury resorts | Couples, restorative trips | 2.5 million VND and up |
| **Non Nuoc** | Calmer, near Marble Mountains | Longer stays, golfers | 1.2–4.5 million VND |

Staying at My Khe is convenient but not atmospheric; Hai Chau gives you the river, the markets and the food streets within walking distance, with the beach a 10-minute ride away.

## What to eat, and where the locals eat it

- **Mi Quang** — turmeric noodles with a spoonful of broth, shrimp, pork and peanuts, plus a fistful of herbs. 30,000–50,000 VND.
- **Bun cha ca** — fish-cake noodle soup in a clear, slightly sweet broth; the Da Nang breakfast.
- **Banh xeo** — the central Vietnamese crispy pancake, folded with shrimp, pork and bean sprouts, wrapped in rice paper with herbs.
- **Seafood** — pick from the tank, agree the price by weight first. Grilled scallops with spring onion are the local benchmark.
- **Banh canh** — thick tapioca noodles with fish cake and crab, sold from mid-afternoon.
- **Coffee** — iced milk coffee, coconut coffee and, everywhere now, salted coffee. The city takes its caffeine seriously.

Three streets worth walking hungry: Nguyen Chi Thanh for seafood, Hoang Dieu for bun cha ca, and the small lanes around Con Market for everything else.

## Practical notes

- **Money**: cards work in hotels and malls; street food, markets and taxis are cash. Keep 20,000, 50,000 and 100,000 VND notes.
- **Transport**: install both Grab and Be — prices differ by time of day. The airport is 3 km away; a metered taxi or Grab ride to My Khe costs less than 100,000 VND.
- **Sun**: the UV here is serious even when overcast. Reef-safe sunscreen, and reapply after swimming.
- **Ba Na Hills**: 950,000 VND for adults, 750,000 VND for children between 1 m and 1.39 m, including the cable car and most rides. Weekends and Vietnamese holidays double the queue.
- **Dress**: pagodas and the Lady Buddha require covered shoulders and knees. Carry a light scarf.
- **Typhoon season**: October and November can bring cancellations of boats and cable cars. Keep one flexible day in the plan.

## FAQ

**How many days do I need in Da Nang?**
Three nights covers the beach, the mountain, Ba Na Hills and the dragon show without rushing. Four lets you add Hoi An or My Son.

**Is the Golden Bridge worth the ticket price?**
Yes if you go early and treat it as part of a full Ba Na day. No if you expect a quiet monument — it is a theme park, and the ticket includes the whole complex.

**When exactly does the dragon breathe fire?**
At 21:00 on Friday, Saturday and Sunday, plus major public holidays. Fire lasts about two minutes, water about three.

**Which beach should I book?**
My Khe for convenience and restaurants, Non Nuoc for calm, Son Tra for quiet and greenery.

**Do I need a visa or permit for Hoi An?**
No. Since the 2025 merger, Hoi An is part of Da Nang city administratively, and it is a 30–40 minute drive from the centre.

**Is Da Nang good for families?**
Very. Shallow, lifeguarded beaches, short transfer times, the cable car and Fantasy Park, and food that is not too spicy for children.
