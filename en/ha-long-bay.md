# Ha Long Bay: Choosing the Boat That Decides Your Trip

Nearly 2,000 limestone islands push out of the water in Quảng Ninh, about 170 km east of Hanoi. The landscape is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it is genuinely one of the most photographed places in Asia — which is also the problem. Between the day-trippers, the tour buses and the boat traffic, the bay can feel less like a natural wonder and more like a queue with a view.

The fix is not complicated, but it is specific: sleep on the water, choose your route by how crowded it is rather than by how cheap it is, and understand what each stop actually asks of you. This Ha Long Bay cruise guide covers those decisions.

> **Administrative update (2026):** Quảng Ninh became a centrally-run city on 1 September 2026, so Ha Long Bay now sits in **Quảng Ninh city** rather than Quảng Ninh province. The bay, the marinas and the cruise routes are unchanged.

## Why an overnight cruise beats a day trip

A day trip from Hanoi means roughly five to six hours of driving for three to four hours on the water, timed to the middle of the day when the bay is busiest and the light is flattest.

An overnight cruise buys you the hours that matter: the bay at 5:30 am with mist sitting between the karsts, the deck at sunset when most boats have gone, and a night anchored in a sheltered cove. It also gets you further out — the quieter corners of Lan Ha and Bái Tử Long are effectively closed to day boats.

If you have exactly one day, go to Lan Ha Bay from Cát Bà instead. If you have two, sleep on the bay.

## What you are actually looking at

The limestone here is hundreds of millions of years old, shaped by sea level changes and erosion into towers, arches and hidden lagoons. Many islands are hollow. Some hold caves big enough to walk through; others are ringed by collapsed chambers that you can only enter by kayak at low tide.

That geology explains the itinerary everyone follows: caves where the rock opens up, lagoons where it closes in, and viewpoints from the top of the same rock.

## 2D1N, 3D2N or a Cát Bà base

| Option | Typical cost per person | What you get | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 days, 1 night (traditional junk) | ~$130–400 | Transfer from Hanoi, one cabin, all meals, kayaking, Sưng Sốt Cave, Ti Tốp, sunset and sunrise on deck | First visits, short trips |
| 3 days, 2 nights (junk or yacht) | ~$250–800 | Everything above plus Bái Tử Long or Lan Hạ, more kayaking, Cát Bà National Park, Việt Hải village | Photographers, slow travellers |
| Cát Bà Island base + day cruises | $30–300 per night + day tour | Land-based hotel, day boat into Lan Hạ, beaches, national park, caves | Families, longer stays, seasickness-prone travellers |

The 2D1N trip is not a compromise so much as a sampler: you will see the two headline stops and almost nothing else. The 3D2N is where the bay stops being a photo opportunity and starts being a place.

A junk and a modern yacht visit many of the same stops. The difference is the cabin, the noise level and the number of people at breakfast — not the scenery.

## The stops, and what they demand

**Sưng Sốt (Surprise Cave)** — the largest cave in the bay, on Bồ Hòn Island. Two vast chambers, roughly 12,000 m² of floor between them, reached by a stone staircase of about 200 steps. Opened to visitors in 1993, recorded by French explorers in 1901. Lighting is theatrical, the route is one-way, and it takes 45–60 minutes. Fitness matters more than any guide admits; there is no lift and no shortcut.

**Ti Tốp Island** — named after Soviet cosmonaut Gherman Titov, who visited in 1962. A crescent beach at the base, then roughly 400 steps to a summit about 110 m above the water. The climb takes 15–25 minutes depending on the queue on the stairs, and the reward is the classic 360° view. Go early in the stop or late; the middle of the afternoon is shoulder-to-shoulder.

**Luồn Cave** — a low limestone arch you enter by kayak or rowboat, opening into a enclosed lake ringed by cliffs. This is the closest the bay gets to silence. Kayaks are provided on almost every cruise; tandem boats are standard.

**Floating villages** — Cửa Vân and Vung Viêng were home to generations of fishing families. Most residents were resettled on land from 2014 onward under a government programme, and what remains is partly a working aquaculture site, partly a preserved cultural stop. Visit it as a village, not a human zoo: ask before photographing people, buy directly if you want pearls, and skip the stop entirely if the framing makes you uncomfortable.

## Beyond the main bay: Lan Ha and Bái Tử Long

**Lan Hạ Bay**, on the Cát Bà side, covers about 7,000 hectares with some 400 islands — the same geology, a fraction of the boat traffic. Bright Cave and Dark Cave are kayak passages; Ba Trái Đào beach is calm enough to swim. Access is by cruise from Cát Bà or by day boat from Cát Bà town.

**Bái Tử Long Bay**, to the northeast, is where the horizon empties out. Almost no day boats, a handful of overnight routes, and stops like Thiên Cảnh Sơn Cave, Vung Viêng and Bàn Chân beach. This is the version of Ha Long that people describe when they say "you have to see it before it changes."

If you are choosing between the two: Lan Hạ for kayaking and swimming, Bái Tử Long for emptiness.

## When to sail, and when not to

| Season | Months | Conditions | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring | Mar–May | Mild, clear, calm water | Clean light for photographs; passing drizzle |
| Summer | Jun–Aug | Hot, humid, peak domestic season | Afternoon storms; book weekends well ahead |
| Autumn | Sep–Nov | Cool, dry, stable | The best overall window; October is the sweet spot |
| Winter | Dec–Feb | 10–18°C, mist, grey skies | Atmospheric and quiet, but cold on deck |

Avoid the typhoon window from roughly July to September, when cruises are cancelled at short notice. Check the forecast 48 hours out and keep your first and last nights flexible if you are travelling in that period.

## Booking without regret

**Book with the operator, not a reseller.** Bhaya, Paradise, Indochina Junk and Heritage Line all sell directly. Agents add a margin and rarely add flexibility.

**Pick your deck.** Main-deck cabins sit near the engine; upper decks are quieter with better views. A private balcony is genuinely worth the extra for the dawn hours.

**Match the boat to your group size.** Twelve to twenty cabins feels personal and flexible. Forty-plus cabins buys you a spa and a pool, and loses the intimacy.

**Declare dietary needs at booking.** Menus lean heavily on seafood, and every operator can adapt — but only if the galley knows before you board.

**Check what the price excludes.** Bay sightseeing fees, drinks, tips and transfers from Hanoi are the usual extras.

## Pack like the bay demands

- **Dry bag.** Kayak spray reaches everything. Phone, camera, passport.
- **Quick-dry towel and swimwear.** Ti Tốp beach and the kayak landing.
- **Layers.** Deck wind at 6 am is not the same climate as noon.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen, hat, polarised sunglasses.** Water glare is brutal.
- **Motion sickness tablets.** Two and a half hours of bus plus boat swell is a real combination; take them 30 minutes before departure.
- **Small notes for tips.** Crew tips are customary and usually pooled.

## FAQ

### Is 2 days 1 night enough for Ha Long Bay?
It is enough to see the two headline stops and to experience dawn on the water. It is not enough to reach the quiet areas. If the bay is the point of your trip rather than a stop on the way, do 3 days 2 nights.

### Can you swim in Ha Long Bay?
Yes, at designated beaches such as Ti Tốp and Ba Trái Đào, and from boats that anchor in calm coves. Swimming is not allowed at cave stops or in the main shipping channels.

### Is Ha Long Bay too crowded?
The central route is busy, especially between 10 am and 3 pm. Overnight cruises and the Lan Hạ or Bái Tử Long routes are dramatically quieter. Choosing the route matters more than choosing the boat.

### What happens if a cruise is cancelled for weather?
Port authorities close the bay when a typhoon or strong wind warning is issued, and reputable operators refund or reschedule. This is why travel insurance and a flexible final night are worth the small cost.

### Ha Long or Lan Ha Bay?
Same karst landscape. Ha Long has the icons and the crowds; Lan Hạ has the kayaking, quieter water and easier access from Cát Bà. First-timers usually want Ha Long once; repeat visitors go to Lan Hạ.
